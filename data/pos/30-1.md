# Alberta Social Studies 30-1 — Program of Studies

## Course Overview

Social Studies 30-1 explores the complexities of ideologies and how they shape perspectives, governance, and global issues. Students examine the tension between the rights of the individual and collective identity, and critically assess the impact of ideologies on society.

---

## Related Issue 1: Should ideology be the foundation of identity?

### Values and Attitudes
- Appreciate the relationship between ideology and identity
- Recognize how ideologies shape world views

### Knowledge and Understanding
- **1.1** Define ideology and explain its significance in shaping personal and collective identity
- **1.2** Examine the relationship between values, beliefs, and ideological perspectives
- **1.3** Analyze classical and modern liberalism, including the origins and evolution of liberalism in Western thought
- **1.4** Explore the philosophical foundations of liberalism: individualism, freedom, rule of law, human dignity, private property, economic freedom
- **1.5** Examine collectivism and its philosophical foundations: collective identity, common good, state intervention, collective rights
- **1.6** Compare liberal and collectivist ideologies with reference to their impact on individuals and societies

### Skills and Processes
- Analyze ideological perspectives in primary and secondary sources
- Evaluate the influence of ideology on individual and collective decision-making

---

## Related Issue 2: Is resistance to liberalism justified?

### Values and Attitudes
- Appreciate diverse perspectives on liberalism and its alternatives
- Reflect critically on challenges to liberal principles

### Knowledge and Understanding
- **2.1** Examine ideological alternatives to liberalism: conservatism, fascism, communism, socialism, anarchism
- **2.2** Analyze the historical context in which alternatives to liberalism emerged
- **2.3** Assess the impact of non-liberal ideologies on individuals and collectives (e.g., totalitarianism, authoritarianism)
- **2.4** Examine the concepts of left-wing and right-wing ideology on the political spectrum
- **2.5** Analyze how economic systems (capitalism, socialism, communism) reflect ideological perspectives
- **2.6** Evaluate the successes and failures of non-liberal ideologies in the 20th century (e.g., Nazi Germany, Soviet Union, Communist China)

### Skills and Processes
- Interpret political cartoons, propaganda posters, and visual sources to identify ideological perspectives
- Compare and evaluate competing ideological claims using evidence

---

## Related Issue 3: To what extent are the principles of liberalism viable?

### Values and Attitudes
- Value the role of liberal principles in shaping democratic societies
- Consider how liberal principles can be balanced with collective needs

### Knowledge and Understanding
- **3.1** Examine the principles of modern liberalism: pluralism, multiculturalism, human rights, social justice, welfare state
- **3.2** Analyze the tension between individual rights and collective responsibilities in liberal democracies
- **3.3** Assess the impact of globalization on liberal principles
- **3.4** Examine how liberal democracies respond to threats: terrorism, economic inequality, environmental crises
- **3.5** Evaluate the role of international institutions (UN, WTO, NATO) in promoting liberal values
- **3.6** Analyze how technology and media influence the viability of liberal principles

### Skills and Processes
- Assess the viability of liberal principles using contemporary case studies
- Construct persuasive arguments supported by evidence from multiple sources

---

## Related Issue 4: To what extent should my actions as a citizen be shaped by an ideology?

### Values and Attitudes
- Recognize the responsibility of citizens in a liberal democracy
- Appreciate the relationship between ideology, citizenship, and civic participation

### Knowledge and Understanding
- **4.1** Examine forms of citizenship and civic participation in liberal democracies
- **4.2** Analyze the relationship between ideological beliefs and political action
- **4.3** Assess the role of media in shaping ideological perspectives and civic participation
- **4.4** Examine case studies of individuals and groups who took ideologically motivated action (e.g., civil rights movements, environmental activism, political reform)
- **4.5** Evaluate the responsibilities and limitations of ideologically motivated citizenship

### Skills and Processes
- Analyze how ideology motivates civic action using real-world examples
- Develop and defend a position on a contemporary ideological issue

## Diploma Exam Weighting

- **Part A** (Multiple Choice): 50% — Knowledge and understanding, application
- **Part B** (Written Response): 50% — Analysis, evaluation, synthesis across sources

## Key Concepts for Question Writing

Recurring concepts throughout the course:
- Individual rights vs. collective rights
- Political spectrum (left/right)
- Role of the state
- Economic systems and ideology
- Nationalism and identity
- Historical atrocities and ideological justification
- Media, propaganda, and public opinion
- Globalization and its effects on ideology
- Indigenous perspectives and liberalism
- Environmental policy and ideology
